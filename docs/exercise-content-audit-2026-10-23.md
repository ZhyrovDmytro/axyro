Visually inspected four sampled positions across each of the 36 GIFs and eight positions across each of the four MOV clips. This establishes movement correspondence, not frame-by-frame certification of technique or clinical approval. All original media paths and stable IDs remain intact.

Every exercise now has an individual padel explanation in its sport profile. Direct exercise pages default to padel relevance when no valid sport filter is supplied. Existing explicit sport filters still select their own profiles. Added the five previously missing padel profiles (squat, reverse lunge, row, weight shift and march).

## Evidence and interpretation

- [Padel physical-demand review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35409836/) supports the general context of intermittent effort and changes of direction.
- [Padel performance review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38053966/) describes match and stroke demands.
- [ACE exercise library](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/) and [NASM exercise library](https://www.nasm.org/workout-exercise-guidance) provide general movement and muscle-group references.

Exercise-to-padel explanations are practical training inferences from movement demands, not evidence that each exact drill improves match results or prevents injury. Strength and mobility preparation do not substitute for stroke or reaction practice.

## Reference limitations

The three prone videos show plates, a roller or a raised block. They remain executable floor exercises with optional light dumbbells, as required by the catalog contract; the text now explicitly identifies a home adaptation. Row and wrist-extension references similarly show supports that differ from the household setup. The dynamic march and bridge references show relatively high knee lifts; easy pacing and reduced range remain intentional options. GIF speed is not the authoritative workout cadence.
